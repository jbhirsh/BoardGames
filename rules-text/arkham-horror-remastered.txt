OVERVIEW
For eons, the Ancient Ones have lain dormant, leaving
humankind to carry on its existence in blissful ignorance.
But the Ancient Ones have begun to stir in their slumber, and
doom now comes to Arkham.
In Arkham Horror: Remastered, 1–8 players take on the roles
of investigators seeking to prevent the cataclysmic awakening
of an Ancient One. The rising eldritch influence causes tears
in reality, opening gates to Other Worlds and allowing
unspeakable abominations to cross into Arkham.
To save their world, investigators must close all such gates
into the city, or permanently seal enough gates to prevent the
Ancient One’s arrival on Earth. Should they fail to do so, they
will engage in one final battle against the arisen Ancient
One, with the fate of humanity hanging in the balance.

SETUP
To set up the game, resolve the following steps:
1. Set Up Game Board
Place the game board in the center of the play area. Place
the terror token on the “0” space of the terror track.
2. Place Starting Clues
Place 1 clue on each unstable location; unstable locations
are indicated by a red diamond.
3. Create General Supplies
Create general supplies out of the game’s tokens and dice
and place them near the game board.
4. Create Card Decks
Gather each type of card and place them in decks near the
game board.
5. Create Monster Supply
Gather all of the monster tokens and separate out all of the
Mask monsters. Place the rest of the monster tokens into a
bowl, bag, or cup to create the monster supply.
6. Create Gate Stack
Shuffle the gate tokens facedown to
create the gate stack.
7. Determine Ancient One
Randomly select an Ancient One
sheet and place it next to the game board. Alterrnatively,
players may agree to choose which Ancient One they are
going to fight. It is recommended that new players play
against Azathoth for their first game.
8. Set up Ancient One Dashboard
Slide the Ancient One sheet into
the Ancient One dashboard with
the gameplay side of the Ancient
One faceup; this is the side that
has the abilities on the righthand
side of the sheet. Place the green
doom cube on the “0” notch of the
doom track. Resolve any Ancient
One-specific setup instructions.
Set the turn tracker, hit tracker, and player count cubes
aside; these are used during the final battle.
9. Determine Lead Investigator
Choose one player to take the lead
investigator token. Players can select
the lead investigator randomly, or select
the player that has the most experience
playing the game.
10. Determine Investigators
The lead investigator deals each player a random
investigator sheet. Alternatively, players may agree to
choose which investigator they are going to play.

11. Gain Fixed Possessions
Each player gains the possessions listed under the “Fixed
Possessions” header on the back of their investigator sheet,
searching the relevant decks for specific cards. Then,
shuffle all decks that contain randomized cards.
12. Gain Random Possessions
Each player draws cards from the decks indicated under
the “Random Possessions” header on the back of their
investigator sheet and gains them; if an investigator has an
ability that can be used during setup, they can use it here.
13. Place Investigators
Each player finds the investigator stand that matches
their investigator and places it on the game board location
listed under the “Home” header on the back of their
investigator sheet.
14. Set Up Investigator Dashboards
Each player slides their investigator
sheet into their investigator dashboard.
They place their sanity and stamina
cubes into the starting notches of the
sanity and stamina track, respectively.
These notches are also indicated by a
diamond shape in their background.
Place the black maximum sanity and
maximum stamina cubes one notch
above the sanity and stamina cubes. If
an investigator begins the game with
a card that increases their maximum
sanity or stamina, make additional
adjustments the initial setup.
Each player determines their starting attributes by placing
one attribute cube into a notch of their choice in each of
the three attribute columns. Each cube determines the
value of the attribute to its left and its right, with higher
numbers indicating a greater number of dice rolled—and
thus a greater chance of success—and lower numbers
indicated a lesser number of dice rolled.
15. Resolve Starting Mythos Card
The lead investigator draws the top
card of the mythos deck and resolves it
as detailed in the “Phase IV: Mythos”
section on page 21; if the drawn
Mythos card has the Rumor trait or there
is not a gate pictured in its upper-left
corner, discard it and draw again.
16. Begin The Game
The players are now ready to begin the
game.

WINNING THE GAME
The central goal of Arkham Horror is to win the game by
defeating the Ancient One. This can be done in one of three
different ways, as follows; don’t worry if you don’t understand
them now, just keep these in mind as you read the rules.
Close All Gates
If at any point, both of the following conditions are met, the
investigators immediately win the game.
• All gates on the game board
are closed.
• The investigators have
a number of unspent
gate trophies equal to or
greater than the number of
investigators.
Because a variety of game effects can spawn gates , and
because gates typically require multiple turns to close, this can
be a difficult method of winning the game early on. However,
it becomes easier to achieve as more elder sign tokens are
placed on the board. Elder sign tokens prevent gates from
spawning in their spaces, which makes life easier for everyone.
Seal Six Gates
If at any point there are six elder sign tokens
on the board, the investigators immediately
win the game. An elder sign token is placed
when an investigator closes a gate and then,
optionally, spends clues to seal it.
Win the Final Battle
If none of the previous conditions are met, and the Ancient
One awakens, the last chance for the investigators to win the
game is to emerge victorious from the final battle. More than
likely, if the investigators are forced to face the Ancient One in
battle, they will die.

THE ANCIENT ONE
The Ancient One is an agent of the Mythos, and the primary
antagonist in a game of Arkham Horror. It is a horrific
creature that defies the logic of reality, slumbering in the earth
until it can awaken and bring its full power to bear against
humanity.
Persistent Effects
Even while the Ancient One stirs in its slumber, it has an effect
on the game. The front of the Ancient One sheet contains
all of the instructions needed to resolve the Ancient One’s
persistent effects.
Doom
As the game progresses, gates and other game effects will
cause doom to advance. For each point that doom advances,
move the doom cube one notch to the right. Doom serves as
the game's timer, and the investigators have until the doom
track is full to close or seal the requisite number of gates and
win the game. If the doom cube advances to the final space of
the doom track, the Ancient One awakens, and play proceeds to
the final battle (see “The Final Battle” on page 29).
Open Gates
The Ancient One can also awaken if too many gates are open
at once. Investigators must keep this in mind as they play
the game and do their best to keep the number of open gates
under control.
The Ancient One awakens, and play proceeds to the final battle
(see “The Final Battle” on page 29) if the following open
gate thresholds are met:
Investigators The Ancient One Awakens at:
1–2 8 open gates
3–4 7 open gates
5–6 6 open gates
7–8 5 open gates
Worshipper Monsters
Each Ancient One has its own worshippers. Worshipper
monsters gain special strength, often increasing the monster’s
toughness, combat damage, or other abilities.
An Ancient One’s worshipper monsters are shown on its sheet.
If a worshipper monster is drawn, use the printed token back
on the Ancient One sheet when in combat with that monster
instead of the token itself.

CORE CONCEPTS
The following concepts are central to understanding the rules
of the game.
Investigators
During setup, each player sets up an investigator in their play
area. Each investigator is represented by a standee on the
game board, a sheet that shows their abilities, and a dashboard
that tracks their sanity, stamina, and attributes. From here
on, and on all game components, players are referred to as
investigators.
Lead Investigator
The investigator that took the lead investigator token during
setup is the lead investigator. During each upkeep
phase, a different player will become the lead investigator for
that game round. The lead investigator has three main
responsibilities.
1. Goes First in Player Order
The lead investigator token is first in turn order.
2 Resolves General Game Effects
General game effects that are not specific to an investigator
are resolved by the lead investigator.
3. Breaks Ties
When a game effect specifies a target and there is a tie (for
example “the investigator that has the highest current
stamina”), and the players as a group cannot agree, the
lead investigator chooses. Likewise, when multiple general
game effects are resolved in the same timing window, the
lead investigaor chooses which order they are resolved in.
Turn Order
Turn order is the order in which investigators resolve game
effects. When an effect is resolved in turn order, begin with
the lead investigator and proceed clockwise. Most phases in
a game round are resolved in turn order. Additionally, when
resolving a global game effect (such as “Each investigator loses
1 stamina”), that effect is resolved in turn order.
Investigator Sheet and Dashboard
An investigator’s sheet and dashboard detail their sanity,
stamina, attributes, focus, and investigator abilities.
Sanity and Stamina
An investigators sanity and stamina are tracked by blue and
red cubes, respectively, and their initial values are set during
setup. When an investigator loses sanity or stamina, they
move the respective cube down a number of notches equal to
the amount lost.
When an investigator’s sanity is reduced to 0, that investigator
is driven insane. When an investigator’s stamina is reduced
to 0, that investigator is knocked unconscious. If an
investigator’s sanity and stamina are both reduced to 0
simultaneously, that investigator is devoured.
When an investigator recovers sanity or stamina, they
move the respective cube up a number of notches equal to the
amount recovered.
Maximum Sanity and Stamina
Investigators have a default maximum sanity and stamina.
During setup, black cubes are placed to indicated the point
at which the maximums would be surpassed. An investigator
cannot recover sanity or stamina beyond their maximums.
Certain game effects can increase or decrease an investigator’s
maximum values. Maximums can be increased by no more
than 3 beyond an investigator's initial value. Investigators
do not automatically recover sanity or stamina when their
maximums are increased unless otherwise specified.
If an investigator’s maximum sanity or stamina is lowered to
a value below their current sanity or stamina, their current
sanity or stamina is lowered to match that maximum. If an
investigator’s maximum stanity or stamina is reduced to 0,
that investigator is devoured.

Attributes
An investigator’s attributes each represent a different
physical or mental characteristic, which determines how many
dice are rolled when that characterstic is tested. Attribute
cubes are are placed during setup and indicate the current
values of each attribute pair.
Each attribute is tied to another attribute as follows:
• Speed  and Sneak 
• Fight  and Will 
• Lore  and Luck 
Each investigator has a different ranges for their attributes.
While the initial positions of the attribute cubes are decided
during setup, an investigator can spend focus during the
upkeep phase to adjust their attributes as the game progresses.
Many items, allies, and other cards can increase or decrease
an investigator’s attributes. Sometimes, these modifiers are
constantly active. Other times, they are are only applied when
specific conditions are met. These modifiers affect the value of
an attribute but do not move the attribute cube.
Focus
An investigator’s focus value represents their ability to fine-
tune their attributes, as detailed in the "Focus Attributes"
section on page 13. On rare occasions, focus can be spent
on other game effects.
Investigator Abilities
Each investigator has one or more abilities, as detailed on the
right-hand side of their investigator sheet. These abilities are
extremely varied and represent the core of what makes each
investigator unique.

Checks
Many effects require the investigators to make checks. A
check is a method of determining success or failure, and is
based on an attribute of the investigator making the check.
To make a check, an investigator rolls a number of dice equal
to the value of the attribute being checked.
Many checks have positive or negative modifiers. A modifier
indicates how many dice to add or subtract to the check.
If an investigator would roll 0 dice on a check, after applying
modifiers, dice are not rolled and the check is an automatic
failure.
Unless stated otherwise, when making a check, each die
result of 5 or 6 is considered a success. For most checks, if an
investigator rolls at least one success, they pass the check. If
an investigator does not roll any successes on a check, they fail
the check. The effect that instructs an investigator to make the
check will also tell them what to do when they pass or fail.
Investigators can also spend clues to roll additional dice on
checks. See “Clues” on page 25.
Difficult Checks
Some checks require more than 1 success. If a check requires
more than 1 success, the number of successes required is
indicated in a green diamond following the check.
Advanced Checks
There are five types of advanced checks:
• Evasion checks ()
• Combat checks ()
• Horror checks ()
• Spell checks ()
• Gate checks (/)
Each of these checks is derived from an attribute and counts
as a check of that attribute's type, but can also be modified by
effects that support that advanced check specifically. Standard
attribute checks, however, do not gain bonuses from effects
that grant bonuses to advanced checks.
Each advanced check has a special effect and will be covered
in a later section.

Die Rolls
Sometimes, an investigator may be instructed to roll or more
dice to resolve a game effect. These die rolls are not checks
and do not receive bonues from effects that only affect checks.
Results of 5 or 6 when rolling dice are successes, if applicable.
Otherwise, the game will instruct you how to interpret the die
roll and resolve its outcome.
Ready and Exhausted
Cards in an investigators play area can be ready (faceup) or
exhausted (facedown). All cards enter play ready, however,
using their effects may exhaust them. When an effect intructs
an investigator to exhaust a card, they turn it facedown;
it cannot be used again until it is readied, which typically
happens during the upkeep phase.
Discarding, Searching, and Shuffling
When a card is discarded, it is placed on the bottom of its
corresponding deck; if not playing with any expansions,
when a location card is discarded, its corresponding deck
is also shuffled. When a token is discarded, it is returned to
its corresponding supply and, if applicable, that supply is
randomized.
When an effect instructs an investigator to search a deck, the
deck is always shuffled afterward.
Gaining Cards
When an investigator is instructed to gain a specific card
by name (for example, “Search the common item deck for
Whiskey and gain it.”) they search the indicated deck for
that item, add it to their play area, then shuffle the deck. If
the specified card does not belong to a randomized deck (for
example, a Retainer or Bank Loan), the deck does not need
to be shuffled after the card is gained. If a specified card is not
available, it is not gained, and no replacement is drawn in its
place.
When an investigator is instructed to gain a card from a
specific deck, but name of the card is not specified (for
example, “Gain 1 common item”), the investigator draws the
top card of the indicated deck and gains it. Since the deck was
not searched, it is not shuffled afterward.
Loss Vs Cost
During the game, investigators will be instructed to suffer
losses and pay costs. Although the end result is similar, the
underlying mechanics are different. A loss can often be
reduced by cards, investigator abilities, or other game effects.
A cost is paid voluntarily, and its amount cannot be reduced or
mitigated unless specified.
Return to The Box
When a game component is returned to the box, it cannot be
used for the remainder of the game, unless specified.
Counters
Counters are tokens used to track game effects.
They are taken from the supply when placed and
returned to the supply when discarded. Counters
are most commonly placed on cards. Some cards
instruct investigators to add counters to them,
while others enter play with counters already on
them. The specific rules governing counters are
explained on each component that uses them.
Adjacency
Two spaces are adjacent when they are connected by a solid
white or colored line. Two investigators and/or monsters are
adjacent when they are in adjacent spaces. Two neighborhoods
are adjacent when a space in one neighborhood is adjacent to a
space in the other neighborhood. Spaces connected by means
other than solid white or colored lines are not adjacent.

THE GAME ROUND
Arkham Horror is played over a series of game rounds. During
each round, investigators will resolve four phases:
1. Upkeep: Investigators perform game maintenance
steps such as readying exhausted cards and focusing
their attributes.
2. Movement: Investigators move around the board, fight
monsters, and trade items with one another.
3. Encounter: Investigators resolve encounters, use
location abilities, and close or seal gates.
4. Mythos: A Mythos card is drawn. Gates and monsters
can spawn, clues can be placed on the board, and
environments or rumors can come into play.
Each phase is covered in detail in the following sections.
Phase I: Upkeep
During the upkeep phase, investigators perform the following
steps in order.
1. Pass Lead Investigator
The investigator that has the lead investigator token passes
the token to the investigator on their left. That investigator
is now the lead investigator. This step is not resolved
during the game's first upkeep phase.
2. Ready Exhausted Cards
Investigators ready each of their exhausted cards.
“Upkeep:” effects related to readying exhausted cards are
resolved during this step.
3. Gain Focus
Investigators gain focus equal to the focus value on their
investigator sheet. Focus is normally spent during step 5
to focus an investigator's attributes, but occasionally it can
also be spent to resolve various upkeep effects. Unspent
focus does not carry over to the next game round.
4. Resolve Upkeep Effects
Some components have upkeep effects. These
components are indicated by a red upkeep symbol,
and each has the word “Upkeep:” follow by an
effect. Some upkeep effects have additional timing
or conditional restrictions, which are indicated by the
effect itself. Each upkeep effect is resolved only once per
upkeep phase, unless otherwise indicated. Upkeep effects
can be found both on components in an investigator’s play
area and on those in the general play area. They are
resolved in the following order:
4a. Investigator Upkeep
Each investigator resolves
the upkeep effects of
cards in their play area.
4b. Game Upkeep
The lead investigator resolves upkeep effects on general
components, such as Mythos cards, in an order of their
choosing.

attributes. For each point of focus an investigator spends,
they may focus a pair of attributes by moving an attribute

decrease.

giving them a higher chance of success on checks that
use that attribute. Investigators can spend their focus
however they wish. They may focus multiple attributes
once or they may focus the same attribute multiple
times, in any combination, so long as the total number of
notches moved does not exceed their focus. Investigators
who do not wish to focus their attributes are not required

Phase II: Movement
During the movement phase, investigators move around the
game board in turn order. An investigator's movement is
determined by what kind of space they are in.
If an investigator begins their movement in a city space,
they resolve city space movement. If they begin their turn
in an other world space, they resolve other world
movement. If they begin their turn delayed or lost in time and
space, they resolve those effects instead (see page 17).
City Spaces
The city of Arkham consists of nine neighborhoods, each
comprising 2-3 locations that share a border color connected
to a street space. Locations and street spaces are both types
of city spaces, and make up the majority of the spaces on the
game board. Investigators will often spend most of their time
in these spaces.
Street Spaces
Street spaces are labeled with the name of the neighborhood,
have no corresponding encounters, and are often teeming with
monsters. Investigators who end their movement in the streets
often leave themselves vulnerable to attack by flying monsters
from the Sky (see page 22).
Stable Locations
Stable locations represent various businesses and
municipal locales within Arkham. They have
encounters, often have location abilities, and their
encounters are generally—but not always—safer.
Additionally, gates do not open at stable locations.
Unstable Locations
Unstable locations look similar to stable locations, but are
marked with a red diamond beneath their art. Unstable
locations have clues placed on them during setup that
investigators can later pick up. Clues often spawn at unstable
locations. Unstable locations have dangerous encounters
that often offer rarer rewards than those of stable locations.
Additionally, gates can open at unstable locations.
Location Rewards
Each location has two reward icons on it. Black reward icons
indicate a reward that is guaranteed by resolving its location
ability. White reward icons indicate a reward often available
from encounters at that location.

City Space Movement
At the start of an investigator's movement, if they are in a
city space, they gain a number of movement points equal
to their speed. Investigators can spend those movement
points to move along the solid lines that connect city spaces,
with each point spent allowing the investigator to move a
distance of 1 space. While spending movement points to move
between spaces makes up the core of city space movement,
investigators can also use movement effects, evade and
encounter monsters, and pick up clues.
Movement Effects
Some items and other components can only be used during
the movement phase. These components are indicated by
the word “Movement:” followed by an effect. Effects that
modify an investigator's movement points must be resolved
when an investigator gains their movement points. Other
“Movement:” effects, including those that spend movement
points, can be resolved at any time during an investigator’s
turn during the movement phase before their movement ends.
Evading Monsters
When an investgator tries to move out of a space that contains
one or more monsters, they must make an evasion check
against each monster in that space, in an order of their choice.
An evasion check is an advanced sneak ()check, modified
by the awareness value on the front of the monster token.
If an investigator passes an evasion check against each
monster in the space they are attempting to leave, they can
continue spending movement points to move.
If an investigator fails an evasion check against a monster,
they do not move out of its space, they lose all of their
remaining movement points, and their movement ends.
Then, the monster attacks; the investigator loses stamina
equal to the combat damage () on the back of the monster
token, and the investigator is engaged with that monster.
Ending Movement
When an investigator has no further movement points or
“Movement:”effects to resolve, if a game effect instructs an
investigator to end their movement, or if they are engaged
by a monster, their movement ends. When an investigator's
movement ends, they lose any remaining movement points
and cannot resolve any further “Movement:” effects.
When an investigator’s movement ends, they must engage or
evade (see “Evading Monsters” above) each monster in their
space, one at a time in an order of their choice, that they did
not already evade or engage during their movement.
Clues
When an investigator ends their movement in a
space that contains 1 or more clues, they may gain
any number of those those clues. Clues can be used
by investigators to seal gates and to roll more dice
during checks (see "Clues" on page 25).
Open Gates
When an investigator ends their movement in a space that
contains an open gate, after they have defeated or evaded each
monster in that space, they move to the first space of the other
world that matches that gate.4

Other World Spaces
Other Worlds are depicted alongside one edge of the game
board. Investigators can access these Other Worlds by moving
through corresponding gates that can open inside Arkham.
Each Other World is divided into two spaces—a first space
and a second space. When an investigator first enters an Other
World, they are placed in its first space. Later, they will move to
its second space, and from there, they can return to Arkham.
Each Other World is associated with 2-4 colored icons. Each
icon represents a type of encounter that is likely (but not
guaranteed) to be had at that Other World. Red encounters
commonly threaten an investigator's stamina, whereas yellow
encounters are likely to threaten their sanity. Blue encounters
regularly put an investigator's possessions at risk, and green
encounters offer respite and safety for an investigator passing
through the Other World.
Monsters cannot move into or be placed on Other World
spaces, and monsters that appear during Other World
encounters never persist beyond the encounter itself.
Other World Movement
At the start of an investigators movement, if they are in an
Other World space, they do not gain movement points, and
cannot resolve "Movement:" effects that grant or spend
movement points. An investigator that starts their movement
in the first space of an Other World moves to the second space
of the Other World; if they were already in the second space of
the Other World, they return from the other world
instead.
Returning From Other Worlds
When an investigator moves from the second space of an
Other World back to Arkham, or when a game effect instructs
them to return from an other world, they move to any open
gate that matches their current Other World and place an
explored token beneath their investigator standee with it’s
safe side faceup.
When an investigator would return from the other world, but
there is no open gate that matches their current Other World,
they are instead lost in time and space; they move to Lost in
Time and Space and are delayed.
Explored Token
An investigator that has an explored token beneath their
standee is not automatically drawn through the open gate
in their space. This allows them to attempt to close the gate
during the encounter phase (see "Closing Gates" on page
20).
While the safe side of the explore token is faceup, that
investigator is not required to evade or engage monsters in
their space unless they choose to. During the next round's
upkeep phase, however, the investigator must flip the explored
token in their space to its danger side; that investigator is
still is not drawn through the gate, but does have to evade
or engage each monster in their space during the movement
phase.
If an investigator on an explored token leaves the space with
the gate for any reason, they discard the explored token.

Delayed Investigators
Some game effects instruct an investigator to become delayed,
which hinders their ability to move. When an investigator is
delayed, they place their investigator standee on its side.
If an investigator is delayed at the start their movement,
regardless of what type of space they are in, they return their
investigator standee to its upright position; they are no longer
delayed, but their movement ends.
If an investigator stands up from being delayed in the Arkham
Jail Cell, they also move to the Police Station.
Lost in Time and Space
Some game effects instruct an investigator to become lost
in time and sapce, which traps them in the space between
realities. When an investigator is lost in time and space, they
move to Lost in Time and Space and become delayed.
If an investigator in Lost in Time and Space at the start their
movement is not delayed, they move to a city space of their
choice, then resolve their movement as normal; investigators
in Lost in Time and Space that are delayed return their
investigator to its upright position and end their movement.
Trading Possessions
If at any point during the movement phase, two ore more
investigators are in the same space as one another, they can
freely exchange money and cards with the “Item” tag. Cards
that do not have the "Item" tag cannot be traded.

Engaging Monsters in Combat
Monsters are an important presence in every game of Arkham
Horror, and investigators will often have to deal with them—
whether because monsters block a street the investigator
needs to cross, stand on a gate they need to go through, or
simply because they move into an investigator’s space.
An investigator becomes engaged with a monster in their
space when they fail an evasion check, when instructed a
specific game effect, or simply by choosing to engage it. An
investigator cannot become engaged with a monster in a
different space.
When an investigator becomes engaged with a monster, they
flip the monster token to its back side and enter combat. To
resolve a combat, perform the following steps in order:
1. Horror Check
First, the investigator attempts to hold on to their sanity by
making a horror check against the monster. A horror
check is an advanced will () check, modified by the
monster's horror rating. If the investigator fails, they
lose sanity equal to the monster's horror damage ().
Whether they pass or fail, if they are not driven insane
(see "Defeated" on page 23) by losing all of their sanity,
they move on to step 2.
Investigators do not make horror checks against monsters
that have a dash in place of a horror rating.
2. Fight or Flee
The investigator then chooses whether to fight the monster
or flee.
• If the investigator chooses to fight the monster, they
make a combat check against the monster. A combat
check is an advanced fight () check, modified by the
monster's combat rating.
Additionally, when an investigator makes a combat
check, they may choose up to two hands worth of items
(as indicated in the lower-right corner of an item) to use
in the combat. Items not occupying a hand do not grant
fight () bonuses to the investigator, though their other
effects can still be used.
To pass a combat check, an investigator must roll a
number of successes equal to or greater than the monster’s
toughness. If they pass, combat ends; they defeat the
monster and claim its token as a monster trophy.
• If the investigator choose to flee from the monster,
they make an evasion check. An evasion check is an
advanced sneak ()check, modified by the awareness
value on the front of the monster token. If they pass, the
investigator successfully flees and is no longer engaged
with that monster.
If the investigator fails to defeat or evade the monster, they
move on to step 3

3. Take Damage
If the investigator fails a combat or evasion check against
a monster they are engaged with, they lose stamina equal
to the monster's combat damage (). If they are not
knocked unconscious (see "Defeated" on page 23) by
losing all of their stamina, they move on to step 4.
4. Next Combat Round
If the monster was not defeated or evaded, the investigator
returns to step 2 and repeats the steps of combat until either
the investigator or monster is victorious.
Phase III: Encounter
During the encounter phase, each investigator resolves an
encounter, in turn order. Encounters vary greatly, but often
instruct an investigator to make a check, offering rewards
for success and penalties for failure. Some encounters
offer a choice. If an investigator “may” do something in an
encounter, it is optional, and the investigator can choose not
to participate.
The type of encounter an investigator resolves is based on
their current space:
Streets
An investigator in a street space does not resolve an encounter
during the encounter phase unless a specific game effect instructs
them to do so.
Locations
An investigator in a location that does not have an open gate
draws an location encounter from the deck that matches their
current neighborhood. Then, they read and resolve the
encounter that corresponds to their location.
Location Ability
Some locations have a location ability, often beginning
with the phrase “As an encounter...”. An investigator in a
location that has a location ability may choose to resolve
that location ability instead of drawing an encounter. An
investigator cannot, however, forego an encounter in order to
use a location ability that they cannot fully resolve.

Closing Gates
An investigator that has an explored token in a location that
has an open gate does not resolve a location encounter or
location ability. Instead, they make a gate check to attempt
to close the gate. A gate check is an advanced fight () or
lore ()check—investigator's choice—adjusted by the gate
check modifier on the gate that is to be closed.
If the investigator passes the check, the gate is closed and the
investigator claims it as a trophy. Additionally, each monster
on the game board that has the same dimension symbol
(#, *, +, <, [, \, ^, c, o) as the closed gate is discarded
and returned to the monster supply.
If the investigator fails the gate check, the gate is not closed.
The investigator can attempt another gate check during the
next encounter phase, provided that they do not leave the
space with the gate, as that would cause the explored token to
be dicarded.
Sealing Gates
When an investigator closes a gate, they may
optionally choose to seal the gate. To
seal a gate, an investigator spends 5 clues to
place an elder sign token in that gate’s
space. Gates cannot open in a space that
contains an elder sign token.
Other World
An investigators in an Other World space draws an Other
World encounter. Other World encounters are color-coded.
If the drawn card does not match any of the colors associated
with the investigator's current Other World, they discard
that card and draw another one, repeating this process until a
matching Other World encounter is drawn.
Then, if their current Other World matches one of the
encounter headers on the drawn Other World card, they
resolve that encounter. Otherwise, they resolve the encounter
under the “Other” header.
Returning from Other Worlds
Other World encounters might instruct an investigator to
return from the other world, see "Returning from Other
Worlds" on page 16.
“A Monster Appears!”
When an encounter informs an investigator that a monster
appears, they draw a monster from the supply and must either
evade or engage it (see “Evading Monsters” and “Engaging
Monsters in Combat” on pages 15 and 18). This monster
does not count against the monster limit.
If a monster that appears during an encounter is defeated, it is
claimed as a trophy as normal. If it is evaded, or if it defeats the
investigator, it is discarded; it does not remain on the board.

When an encounter instructs an investigator to spawn a gate
in their space, they follow all of the rules for spawning a gate as

Phase IV: Mythos
During the Mythos phase, the lead investigator draws a card
from the Mythos deck and resolves its effects in order, top to
bottom, left to right.
1. Spawn Gate
If a location is indicated in the upper-left corner of the Mythos
card, and there is not already a gate or an elder sign in that
space, spawn 1 gate there by following these steps in order:
1. Draw a gate from the top of the gate stack and place it
faceup in the indicated space.
2. Discard each clue from that space.
3. Advance doom by 1.
4. Each investigator in the gate’s space is drawn
through the gate. They move to the first space of the
corresponding Other World and are delayed.
5. Spawn monsters in the gate’s space based on player
count (1 monster for 1-4 players, 2 monsters for 5-8
players). If a monster would spawn, but the monster
limit has already been reached, that monster is placed
in the Outskirts instead (refer to the corresponding
sections on page 28 for a full explanation of the
monster limit and the Outskirts).
If there is an elder sign token in the indicated location, do not
spawn a gate; the seal prevents a gate from spawning.
If there is already an open gate in the indicated location,
do not resolve any of the the above steps; instead, resolve a
monster surge.
If the Mythos card does not indicate a location, instead resolve
whatever instructions are in its place.
Monster Surge
To resolve a monster surge, draw a number of monsters
equal to the number of players or the number of open gates,
whichever is greater, without looking at them. Place one
monster on each space that has an open gate. If any monsters
remain to be placed, they are placed one at a time on the open
gates, starting with the surging gate and distributing them as
evenly as possible among all open gates. If the monster limit
is reached, each remaining monster is placed in the Outskirts.
(see "Outskirts" on page 28).
2. Place Clues
Place 1 clue in each spaces indicated by clue icons in the
upper right of the Mythos card; if the indicated space has a
gate in it, do not place that clue.
If a clue is placed in a space with an investigator, that
investigator gains the clue; if there are two or more
investigators in the space, they choose which investigator
gains the clue.
If the Mythos card indicates other tokens in this section—
such as activity and closed tokens—place those tokens in
the indicated spaces. If an activity token is placed on the
board, place a second copy of that activity token on this
Mythos card as a reminder.c +

Activate each monster that has a dimension symbol (#,
*, +, <, [, \, ^, c, o) that matches an symbol on
the Mythos card's monster activation bar, following the
activation rules for its monster type.
Monster Types
Each monster type has a unique behavior when activated.
A monster’s type is indicated by the color and pattern of its
border. Monsters that don't move when they activate can
still be moved by other game effects.
Black Border—Normal: Normal monsters
move 1 space along the indicated movement path.
Yellow Border—Stationary: Stationary
monsters have no activation effect.
Red Border—Fast: Fast monsters move 2
spaces along the indicated movement path; if they move
into a space with an investigator, they stop moving.
Orange Border—Special: Special monsters
do not move when they activate. Instead, resolve the
activation effects on the back of their monster tokens.
Blue Border—Flying: If a flying monster is in
a city space when it activates, it moves to an investigator
in an adjacent space; if there are none, it moves to the Sky
instead. If a flying monster is in the Sky when it activates,
it moves to an investigator in any street space; if there
are none, it remains in the Sky. When there are multiple
investigators that a flying monster could move to, it favors
the investigator that has the lowest sneak ().
Moving Monsters
When an activated monster moves, it moves is based on
which side of the activation bar its symbol appears on.
Monsters that have a symbol that matches a symbol on
white side of the activation bar move along the white
movement paths, following the white arrows. Monsters
that have a symbol that matches a symbol on the black
side of the activation bar move along the black movement
paths, following the black arrows.
If a monster is in a space with investigator when it activates,
it does not move. If a monster moves into a space with an
investigator, there is no immediate effect; that investigator
will, however, likely have to evade or engage that monster
during the next movement phase.
4. Resolve Mythos Effect
Resolve the main text of the drawn Mythos card. There are
three main types of Mythos cards:
Headlines
Headlines have one-time
effects. They are resolved and
then the card is discarded,
unless otherwise indicated.
Environments
Environments have persistent
effects. After resolving
the card, place it near the
Mythos deck as a reminder
that its effects are active.
Environments only affect
investigators in city spaces.
There can only be one
Environment in play at a time;
if an Environment is drawn
and there is already one in
play, discard the previous one.
Rumors
Rumors have persistent
effects. After resolving the
card, place it near the Mythos
deck as a reminder that its
effects are active.
Rumor effects persist until the Rumor is passed or failed.
Each Rumor has its own rules and conditions, as indicated
by its text, and often have upkeep effects. Ignoring a
Rumor will often lead to its failure and spell doom for the
investigators—successfully passing them, however, can
yield powerful rewards.
There can only be one Rumor in play at a time; if a Rumor
is drawn and there is already one in play, resolve its other
instructions normally, but discard it without resolving its
Mythos effect.
End of Round
After the Mythos phase is resolved, the round ends, and play
moves to the next round, beginning with the upkeep phase.c

THE INVESTIGATORS
The investigators are a group of unique characters who
have banded together to face the Mythos. Game effects and
components that affect the investigators—both bad and
good—are explained in detail in the following sections.
Negative Statuses
Unfortunately, many terrible things can befall an investigator as
they adventure in Arkham. These awful events are as follows:
Drawn Into The Other World
If at any point outside of the
movement phase an investigator
suddenly finds themselves in
a space that contains a gate,
they are drawn into the other
world; they move to the first
space of the Other World that
corresponds to the gate and
become delayed.
Delayed
When an investigator becomes
delayed, they place their
investigator standee on its side.
That investigator effectively loses
their next movement phase.
If an investigator is delayed
at the start their movement,
regardless of what type of space
they are in, they return their
investigator standee to its upright position;
they are no longer delayed, but their movement ends.
Lost in Time and Space
Some game effects instruct an
investigator to become lost in
time and sapce, which traps them
in the space between realities.
When an investigator is lost in
time and space, they move to
Lost in Time and Space and
become delayed.
If an investigator in Lost in
Time and Space at the start their
movement is not delayed, they move to a city space of their
choice, then resolve their movement as normal; investigators
in Lost in Time and Space that are delayed return their
investigator to its upright position and end their movement.
Defeated (Insane or Unconscious)
When an investigator’s sanity or stamina
reach zero, they are driven insane or knocked
unconscious, respectively, and are defeated.
When an investigator is defeated, they perform
the following steps:
• They discard half of their clues, rounded down.
• They discard half of their items, rounded down.
• They discard their Retainer, if they have one.
Then, they resolve additional steps based on their current
space and whether they are insane or unconscious.
• If they were driven insane in a city space, they recover
1 sanity move to Arkham Asylum; they do not resolve
any further encounters during this round.
• If they were knocked unconscious in a city space, they
recover 1 stamina and move to St. Mary’s Hospital; they
do not resolve any further encounters during this round.
• If they were defeated in an Other World, they recover 1
sanity if they were driven insane or recover 1 stamina if
they were knocked unconscious. Then, they are lost in time
and space.
Devoured
When an investigator's sanity and stamina reach zero at the
same time, they are devoured. Game effects can also cause
an investigator to become devoured. Devoured investigators
perform the following steps:
1. They discard all of their cards, money, clues, and any
tokens other than monster and gate trophies, which
they keep for their next investigator.
2. They shuffle their investigator into the unused
investigators.
3. If the final battle is not active, they replace their
investigator with a new investigator drawn according to
the selection rules established during setup. Then, they
follow standard setup rules for their new investigator,
but do not yet place their standee on the board.
4. At the start of the next upkeep, they place their
investigator into play on their starting space, resuming
play as normal. If an investigator is devoured during
the round in which the Ancient One awakens, their
replacement still enters play at the beginning of the first
ready step of the final battle (see page 29).5

Arrested
Some effects can cause an investigator to be arrested. When an
investigator is arrested, they perform the following steps:
1. The investigator discards half of their money,
rounded down.
2. The investigator moves to the Arkham Jail Cell and
become delayed.
Investigators in the Jail Cell do not resolve encounters
during the encounter phase. When an investigator that was
delayed in the Jail Cell returns their investigator standee to
its upright position, they move to the Police Station and end
their movement; they can have an encounter there during the
encounter phase.
Items, Money, and More
As the game progresses, an investigator’s play area will be
filled with a variety of cards, most of them beneficial, that can
help them fight back against the Mythos.
Money
Money can be spent when prompted during
encounters, or when resolving location
abilities such as those at the Curiositie
Shoppe or St. Mary’s Hospital, to gain
a variety of benefits. An investigator can
trade money with other investigators in
their space during the movement phase.
Items
Cards with the item tag provide a
myriad of benefits to the investigator
who owns them. Common items,
unique items, and spells are all types
of items. An investigator can trade
items with other investigators in
their space during the movement
phase. Some items have a cost to use, or become exhausted
upon use. Exhausted items do not provide any benefits to
the investigator that owns them, but are typically readied
during the next upkeep phase. Some items have traits, such
as Physical, or Tome. Traits have no inherent effect but are
frequently referenced by other game effects.
Counters
Some cards enter play with counters already on them, as
indicated by the counters icon and a number. When a card
that has a counters icon and a number enters play, place the
indicated number of counters on the card. The card's text itself
will indicate how the counters are used. When an item with
counters no longer has any counters on it, discard it.

Concealed Items
An item with the Concealed cannot be discarded by a game
effect. However, if an investigator is instructed to discard
an item in order to resolve a game effect, they can choose to
discard an item with the Concealed ability.
Additionally, when an investigator discards items as a result
of being defeated, they can count a Concealed item toward
the number of items they must discard; the Concealed item,
however, is not discarded.
Allies
Allies represent unique characters who can join
investigators in their battle against the forces of
the Mythos. When an ally joins an investigator,
they often provide an immediate one-time
benefit. While they remain with that investigator,
they can also provide lasting effects, such as
bonuses to attributes. Allies are not items.
Skills
Skills represent an investigator’s specialized
training, expertise, or natural aptitude. When an
investigator gains a skill, they gain a permanent
passive effect, such as a bonus to an attribute or to
a specific type of check. Skills are not items.
Clues
Clues are a vital resource available to the
investigators. They typically spawn during the
Mythos phase and can be picked up when an
investigator ends their movement in the clue's space.
Game effects—such as spending 5 clues to seal a
gate (see "Closing Gates" on page 20)—often
require an investigator to spend clues. These effects
will instruct the investigators on how the clues are used.
Additionally, after rolling dice during a check, an investigator
can spend 1 clue to roll 1 additional die. They can repeat this
process until they are satisfied with the results or until they run
out of clues. Each spent clue adds 1 die even if the investigator
would normally roll 0 dice on a check. Clues cannot be spent
to add dice to die rolls that are not checks.
Special Cards
During the game, investigators might be
instructed to gain a special card, such as a
Retainer, Bank Loan, Silver Twilight Lodge
Membership, Blessed, or Cursed. These cards
represent particular circumstances, privileges,
or obligations that can alter the way investigators
interact with the game. An investigator cannot
have more than one copy of each special card.
Special cards do not need to be shuffled.
Role Cards
Roles represent special positions an investigator
can hold which grant them useful abilities. When
an investigator gains a role card, they take also
gain all cards associated with that role.
Only one investigator can hold each role at a
time. If an investigator is instructed to gain a role
already held by another investigator, they do not
gain that role or any of its associated cards.

Casting Spells
Spells represent magical knowledge investigators
can use to produce a variety of powerful effects.
Unlike other items, however, spells must be
successfully cast before their effects can be
resolved.
To cast a spell, an investigator must first pay its
sanity cost, indicated on the right-hand side of the spell.
If paying this cost does not drive the investigator insane, they
an then make a spell check. A spell check is an advanced
lore () check, modified by the spell's casting modifier,
indicated on the left-hand side of the spell. If they pass, they
resolve the spell’s effect. Whether the investigator passes or
fails the spell check, the spell is exhausted, and any hands
used to cast the spell during combat remain occupied.
Ritual Spells
Ritual spells are powerful, one-time use magicks. After a
Ritual spell is successfully cast and its effect is resolved,
discard it.
Magical Effects
Some spells instruct investigators to gain a specific magical
effect card when the spell is successfully cast.
All copies of each magical effect are identical; they do not
need to be shuffled. An investigator cannot have more than
one copy of each magical effect.
A magical effect is a representation of a spell’s lingering
effect that remains active even while the spell is exhausted.
Unless otherwise noted, magical effects are discarded at the
start of the upkeep phase; they are also discarded if their
corresponding spells are discarded, traded away, or otherwise
lost for any reason.
Hands During Combat
Each item that grants fight () bonuses during combat has
a hand icon in its lower-right corner—either a 1-hand icon or
a 2-hand icon. When an investigator makes a combat check,
they choose up to two total hands worth of items to use during
the check. Items that have hand icons which are not being
used do not grant fight () bonuses, though other effects on
those items can still be used. Items that do not require hands
are not restricted; any number can be used.
Investigators can choose different weapons for each combat
check, even within the same combat. While most bonuses
only affect the combat check being made, some have a longer
duration, such as “until the end of this combat”. Items with a
fixed duration occupy the indicated number of hands across
multiple rounds of combat, until either their duration expires
or until the investigator chooses to use a different item.
Gate and Monster Trophies
An investigator can spend monster and gate trophies collected
during the game to pay for specific effects, such as location
abilities. Monster trophies typically have worth assigned to
them based off of their toughness values. Unspent trophies
are worth points when scoring at the end of the game.
An investigators claims a monster token as a trophy when they
defeat that monster or when they are instructed to by an effect;
they do not gain the trophy when a monster is discarded.
An investigator claims a gate token as a trophywhen they close
a gate, or when they are instructed to by an effect; they do not
gain the trophy when a gate is discarded.
Tokens that have Endless are not claimed as trophies.

THE MONSTERS
Each monster in Arkham Horror has a monster type that
determines what it does when it activates (see “Monster
Types” on page 22). In addition, some monsters have one or
more monster abilities, which are detailed below.
Ambush
While an investigator is engaged with a monster that has
Ambush, they cannot flee from it. Combat continues until
either the monster or the investigator is defeated.
Endless
When an investigator passes a combat check against a monster
that has Endless, they do not claim it as a trophy; when it is
defeated it is discarded.
Physical and Magical Resistance
When an investigator makes a combat check against a monster
that has Physical or Magical Resistance, the investigator's
Physical or Magical items, respectively, provide only half of
their normal fight () bonus, rounded up.
Physical and Magical Immunity
When an investigator makes a combat check against a monster
that has Physical or Magical Immunity, the investigator's
Physical or Magical items, respectively, do not provide their
normal fight () bonus.
Nightmarish X
When an investigator passes a horror check against a monster
that has Nightmarish, they lose X sanity, where X is that
monster’s Nightmarish value.
Overwhelming X
When an investigator passes a combat check against a
monster that has Overwhelming, they lose X stamina, where
X is that monster’s Overwhelming value.
Undead
The Undead ability has no inherent mechanical effect, but can
be referenced by other game effects.
Mask Monsters
Mask monsters are added to the monster supply only
when Nyarlathotep is the Ancient One. They can be
identified by the presence of a mask icon on the front
of the monster token.
Empty Monster Supply
If a monster would be drawn, but the monster supply is empty,
the Ancient One awakens.

THE GAME BOARD
The game board represents the city of Arkham and tracks
many key elements and locations. Understanding it is vital to
fending off the Mythos.
The Outskirts
There is a maximum number of monsters that can be placed in
Arkham at any given time. This is called the monster limit,
and is equal to the number of investigators plus three. When
a monster would be placed on the game board, if the number
of monsters across all city spaces and the Sky is already at the
limit, that monster is placed in the Outskirts instead.
The Outskirts is not a city space. Monsters do not move to
or from the Outskirts. There is also a maxiumum number of
monster that can be in the Outskirts at any given time. This
is called the outskirts limit, and is based on the number of
investigators, as follows:

The Sky
Flying monsters can move to and from the Sky. Monsters in
the Sky count against the monster limit. Investigators cannot
move to or be placed in the Sky unless specified. The Sky is not
a city space.
The Terror Track
Terror is the measure of growing panic spreading across
Arkham. Monsters overrunning the Outskirts and many
other game effects can cause terror to advance. When that
happens, resolve the following steps in order:
1. Move the terror token one space forward along the
terror track.
2. Return the top card of the ally deck to the game box;
that ally has left town and is no longer available.
When terror advances multiple times at once or directly to a
set number, resolve each individual increase separately.
When terror advances to certain thresholds, additional effects
are triggered, and certain locations can close:
• When terror reaches 3, close Schoffner’s General Store.
• When terror reaches 6, close the Curiositie Shoppe.
• When terror reaches 9, close Ye Olde Magick Shoppe.
• When terror reaches 10, Arkham is overrun. Monsters
are no longer placed in the Outskirts; instead, when
there are a number of monsters in Arkham equal to twice
the monster limit, the Ancient One awakens.
When a location is closed, the lead investigator places a closed
token on it. Then, each investigator and monster in that space
moves to the street. Investigator and monster cannot move
to or be placed on a closed location; if an investigator or
monster would be placed on a closed location and there is no
alternative, place them in its connected street space instead.

THE FINAL BATTLE
When all else fails and the Ancient One awakens, the
investigators have one last chance to win: confronting and
defeating the Ancient One in a final, desperate battle. The
Ancient One can awaken when any of the following occur:
• Doom advances to the final notch of the doom track.
• There are too many open gates, based on player count
(see "Open Gates" on page page 7).
• Terror has reached 10 and the number of monsters on
the board is twice the monster limit.
• A game effect says that the Ancient One awakens.
• A gate or monster would spawn but there are none in
their respective supplies.

devoured; they do not take part in the final battle.
