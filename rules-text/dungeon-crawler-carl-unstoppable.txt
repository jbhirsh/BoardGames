SAFETY WARNING: The Borant Corporation kindly requests that you follow the instructions on the
“Welcome, Crawler!” handout provided to all crawlers before you read any further. In addition, there
have been reports of some crawlers using pages torn from this document to circumvent the “one roll of
toilet paper per floor” policy. Use of this product as bathroom tissue is strictly prohibited.
This version of Unstoppable is set in the 18-floor World Dungeon of the Dungeon Crawler Carl novel.
Fight your way through mobs and bosses as Carl or his talking cat. Play solo or bring along a friend for a
co-op crawl!
With this card-crafting system, each card is a sleeve with swappable parts. You’ll craft and customize
your cards as you play! The power curve in this “momentum builder” gives you a fighting chance against
the threats you’ll face.
Explore a single floor of the World Dungeon each time you play. This box contains the first three floors.
In true roguelite fashion, however, only a few benefits that you purchase with XP will carry over to the
next floor!
The goal of the game is to survive one floor of the World Dungeon each time you play, eventually
reaching the stairs and exiting to the next floor.
Each floor is different and has its own method for going down the stairs. Pay attention to the specifics
of the floor to see what a successful exit requires.
But don’t forget that the other part of the goal is survival! The World Dungeon is practically bursting at
the seams with threats and danger.
• You win the game if… all players successfully go down the stairs.
• You lose the game if… your health or your teammate’s health is reduced to zero.

This game features two different play modes: campaign mode and arcade mode.
Campaign mode involves several linked sessions as you crawl the floors one at a time, and certain
choices persist from one game to the next. Arcade mode, on the other hand, lets you crawl a single floor
in a standalone session.
The intention is for new players to experience campaign mode first. The main portion of this rulebook therefore
treats campaign mode as the default. The changes for arcade mode can be found later on (see p. 22).
Note: This section presents the game setup in several smaller stages. Campaign setup is only required for the
first game of a new campaign, while supply setup, crawler setup, and floor setup must be done every time.

1. Floor Mat: Find the floor mat that matches the current floor and is labeled for Arcade/Campaign
Mode in the top right corner. This has the Setup instructions for this floor. Place it on the table.
It will be referenced later.
2. Dungeon Deck: Find either the boss cards (Floors 1–2) or quest cards (Floor 3) for the current
floor. Set these cards next to the floor mat for now.
3. Threat Deck: Find the threat cards for the current floor and the additional threat cards. Shuffle
both sets separately, then stack them, with the additional threats at the bottom. Place this threat
deck next to the core decks, threat side down.
4. Floor Mat Setup: Read and follow the floor specific Setup directions on the floor mat from step 1.
5. Flip Floor Mat: Turn the floor mat from step 1 to the other side. This should say “Campaign Mode”
in the top corner and have a Danger Level track.
6. Danger Marker: Find the danger marker. Place it on the topmost space of the danger track on the
floor mat.
7. Difficulty Level: Choose a difficulty level: NORMAL, HARD, or EXPERT. For a near-impossible
challenge, there is also UNBEATABLE difficulty. This will determine how quickly the danger
marker advances during play.
8. The Stairs: Place 1 stairs card per player next to the floor mat.
9. Floor Mat Achievements: Find the other Floor Mat for the current floor. Place it on the table
with the Campaign Mode side face up, showing the list of possible Achievements for this floor.
Familiarize yourself with these achievements before playing and try to earn them!
B - Floor Setup
1. Core Decks: Divide the 100 unsleeved core cards into 6 decks by level number, shown in the bottom
right corner. Shuffle each deck individually. Place these decks in a face-up row on the table,
arranged left to right in ascending order.
2. Upgrade Deck: Shuffle the 36 unsleeved upgrade cards into a single deck. Place this deck face down
on the table. Reveal the top 6 cards of the deck and place them in a face-up row next to the deck.
Note: These cards have 2 different cutouts, but they should still be shuffled together.
3. Token Supply: Create a supply with the gold tokens and damage tokens, sorted by type and
denomination.

CARD ANATOMY
There are 3 main card types: cores, threats, and
upgrades. Each sleeved “card” that you craft during
play consists of 1 core, 1 threat, and up to 2 upgrades.
1. Cost: How many action points
must be spent to play the card.
Remove action point discs from
your player mat when spent.
2. Stat: Which of the main stats the card is affiliated
with. Many game effects and keywords are linked
to these different stat symbols.
3. Subtype: Whether the card is a tactic or an ally.
Tactics are discarded after use, while allies remain
in play. Some cards have additional subtypes.
4. Effects: What the card does when played or
used. Some cards have a single effect, while
others have multiple effects.
5. Level: Which core deck the card comes from (1–6).
Character cards and starting cards count as level-0
core cards.
Cores make up the front side of sleeved
cards. They represent the powers,
resources, and support available to you.

Threat Cards (93x)
1. Attack: How much damage the threat deals to you
each turn while it remains in play on the battlefield.
2. Defense: A value that is added to the threat’s
attack value to determine its health. You must deal
damage to a threat equal to its health to defeat it.
3. Bonus: A one-time bonus that you gain when you
defeat the threat. Not all threats have a bonus.
4. Keyword: A keyword that links to an ability the
threat has. Not all threats have a keyword.
5. Looming: Some boss threats are Looming.
Looming threats do not engage unless attacked.

The game takes place over a series of turns.
Each turn is broken into 5 phases that are performed in order:
A. Upkeep Phase
B. Draft Phase
C. Main Phase
D. Threat Phase
E. Cleanup Phase
This sequence repeats until you win or lose.
TURN SEQUENCE
1. Races – Resources: New values for your starting gold, health, and mana. These values replace the
default values.
2. Races – Extra Setup: Additional steps to carry out during setup.
3. Classes – Abilities: Beneficial effects with text descriptions. Some classes have a single ability,
while others have multiple.
Races and classes are used for arcade mode and after the first two floors in campaign mode.

The Upkeep Phase is when you carry out various upkeep steps:
1. Gain 1 mana by moving the marker up 1 space on your mana track. If the marker is already at
the top of the track, skip this step. Note: You gain 1 mana even on the very first turn of the game.
2. Place 3 action point discs in the designated space on your player mat. Those action points are
available to be spent in the Main Phase.
3. Refresh all exhausted ally cards in play by rotating them back upright. Those ally cards are
available to be exhausted in the Main Phase.
4. Resolve all active effects that trigger during the Upkeep Phase.
The Draft Phase is when you draft a new card and add it to your hand based on the current position of
your level discs:
Large Level Disc Small Level Disc
1. Find the core deck where your small level disc is located.
2. Draw the top 3 cards of that core deck. Keep 1 of those cards and return the
other 2 to the bottom of the same deck (in any order).
3. Take the top card of the threat deck, without viewing the threat side,
and put your chosen core card into the same sleeve.
4. Add the newly drafted card to your hand with the core side facing you.
5. Reset your small level disc back to where your large level
disc is located and stack them together again.
Note: With 2 players, the process for choosing cards in the Drafting
Phase is slightly different. See 2-PLAYER MODE for further details (p. 20).

The Main Phase is when you can perform various
actions such as use spells, play cards, exhaust
allies, and buy upgrades.
You may take these actions any number of times,
and in any order, as long as you have the cards,
gold, mana, and action points required to do so.
The phase ends when you cannot, or choose not

Many cards have important game effects that are represented by keywords. These keywords are

KEYWORD GUIDE
Focus
Focus effects use an “X” value. The value of X is equal to
the number of cards with a specific listed stat symbol in
your hand and/or in play, including this card itself. Some
Focus effects scale with the value of X, while others
simply need X to meet a certain threshold to trigger.
Example: Make Stuff Go Boom is a level-1 tactic
card with the effect “Focus 3+: 3 .” If you
have 3 or more Strength cards in your hand and/or
in play, including this card itself, the effect deals
3 damage.
Spell
Spell effects use an “X” value. These effects require you
to spend X mana to trigger. If you do not have X mana
available to spend, then you cannot trigger the Spell effect.
Some cards have multiple Spell effects, which you can
trigger and resolve separately by paying their mana costs.
Example: Magic Missile is a starting tactic card
with 3 different Spell effects: Spend 1 mana for 1
damage, spend 2 mana for 2 damage, and spend 3
mana for 3 damage. You may trigger any or all of
these effects individually.
Tough
Tough effects give allies a passive ability. If a Tough ally
would be discarded from play when it has no damage,
it takes 1 damage instead and remains in play. This is a
way to avoid having to discard an ally from play when
you level up or when you defeat a Sacrifice threat.
Example: Chris Andrews is a level-1 ally card with
Tough. If he is in play when you level up, he takes
1 damage instead. If he stays in play until the next
time you level up, he will be discarded from play
then because he has damage.
Unite
Unite effects use an “X” value. The value of X is equal to
the number of unique stat symbols present among the
cards in your hand and/or in play, including this card itself.
Some Unite effects scale with the value of X, while others
simply need X to meet a certain threshold to trigger.
Example: Team-Up Attack is a level-2 tactic card
with a Unite effect to deal X damage. This card is
a Strength card. If you also have Dexterity and
Charisma cards in your hand and/or in play, the

Threat Keywords
Fast threats overrun the battlefield with speed.
Fast threats do not count toward the limit of 3 active threats on the battlefield. When you
add new threats to the battlefield, you must keep adding threats until 3 non-Fast active
threats are on the battlefield and 1 is visible on top of your deck.
LOOMING
Looming threats do not engage unless attacked.
Looming threats enter play rotated vertically. While a threat is Looming, it does not count
as an active threat on the battlefield. When you deal damage to a Looming threat, it rotates
back horizontally and loses the Looming keyword. Some threats have effects that are only
active while the threat is Looming.
RETALIATE
Retaliate threats fight back as they are struck.
When you target a Retaliate threat with an effect that deals damage, it deals you 1 damage
back. The Retaliate effect triggers with each separate damage effect targeting the threat,
even if those effects come from the same card.
SACRIFICE
Sacrifice threats deplete your resources.
When you defeat a Sacrifice threat, you must discard 1 card from hand or 1 ally from play.
If you have no cards in hand, you must discard 1 ally from play, and vice versa. If you have
neither, ignore the Sacrifice effect. In 2-player mode, the Sacrifice effect applies to whoever
defeated the threat, even if it was not their own threat.
Taunt threats command your attention.
When a Taunt threat is on the battlefield or on top of your deck, you must target it with all
effects that deal damage, unless stated otherwise. If there are multiple Taunt threats in
play, you may target any of them. This restriction does not apply to non-damage effects.
Terror threats drain your steely resolve.
When you target a Terror threat with an effect that deals damage, you lose 1 action point
(unless you have none available). The Terror effect triggers with each separate damage
effect targeting the threat, even if those effects come from the same card.
Note: It is possible for a threat to have multiple instances of the same keyword, once the card has
received an upgrade or two, but duplicate keywords do not stack!I have found area-of-effect
spells that do not target, such as “Deal
damage to all active threats,” often evade
the worst of the Dungeon’s defenses.
